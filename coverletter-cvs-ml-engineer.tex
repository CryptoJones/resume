When CVS Health talks about simplifying health care one person, one family, and one community at a time, it lands differently for me than most job postings do. I'm a volunteer firefighter and EMT-B in my small Nebraska town, so caring for people --- often on their hardest day --- isn't an abstraction to me. I'd love to bring the machine-learning systems I build to an organization whose work genuinely reaches people's lives, and the \role\ role on the Caremark team feels like exactly that chance.

I earned that EMT certification during a planned career break, alongside a stretch of deep open-source work --- MCP servers, security fixes to AI frameworks, contributions to OWASP ZAP. Since March 2026 I've been back at full tilt as a contract Principal Software Architect \& ML Engineer for an AI startup, architecting and operationalizing ML pipelines and weaving models into production systems --- and that year confirmed what I want next: a full-time team where I get to do this every day. This role is the heart of that work --- building, deploying, and retraining the enterprise ML products and pipelines that data scientists and the business lean on.

After 25 years of shipping software, I've come to believe the hard part of ML in production is rarely the model itself; it's the platform around it that makes the model dependable. My open-source project \href{https://github.com/CryptoJones/Scylla}{Scylla} is built on that conviction, and I bring the same care to ML pipelines: reproducible training and retraining, deployments people can trust, and quiet vigilance for the data drift that slowly pulls a model away from reality. My everyday toolkit is the modern stack in Python --- RAG pipelines, vector databases, multi-provider model routing, feature engineering, and choosing between models on the metrics that actually move the business. And I'd bring a perspective most ML engineers don't: a security background earned maintaining a hardened fork of the NSA's Ghidra platform. In a place entrusted with people's prescriptions and health data, I care deeply that what we build is trustworthy --- not just accurate, but safe and accountable to the members counting on it.

Honestly, I love building people up as much as building systems. I've grown engineers, mentored through Women in CyberSecurity, and learned to listen hard for what people truly need before writing a line of code. I'm finishing a Master's in Data Analytics to round out the foundation beneath the work I'm already doing, and while the role's telecommuting option fits my long remote track record, I'd gladly come to Irving whenever it would help the team.

I'd be genuinely glad to talk about how I can help CVS Health build machine learning that earns people's trust.
